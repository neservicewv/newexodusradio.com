Water preparedness

Safe drinking water comes first. Plan how your household will store, filter and purify water when the tap stops.

John 4:14

What this guide covers

  • Emergency water storage

    Keep a stored supply for drinking, cooking and basic hygiene. Ready.gov suggests at least one gallon per person per day for several days, plus extra for pets.

  • Water filtration

    How filters work, what they remove and what they don’t, and how to choose one for your household.

  • Water purification

    Boiling and other proven methods for making questionable water safe to drink, and when filtering alone isn’t enough.

  • Rainwater collection

    Collecting rain for non-drinking uses, keeping it clean, and checking your local rules before you start.

  • Water conservation

    Stretching what you have during an outage, and planning priorities for drinking, cooking and sanitation.

  • Emergency drinking water

    Hidden sources already in your home, and how to tell which are safe to use.

  • Safe storage containers

    Food-grade containers, labeling and dating, where to store water, and how often to rotate it.
